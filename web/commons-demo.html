Common-pool resource · agent-based model

Watch a shared resource live or die

Eight agents draw from one renewable pool each round. Whether it is sustained or collapses depends on who is in the group, what they can see, whether anyone enforces a limit, and whether it survives a shock. Pick a regime or adjust the dials, then press run — this is a live, faithful port of the research engine below.

Stock tracks the current round as it plays; Sustainability (final stock ÷ capacity), Fairness, Total harvest and Welfare efficiency are the run's outcome at the end. Welfare efficiency is net payoff (harvest minus monitoring cost) relative to the maximum sustainable yield — 1.0 means the population earned exactly the sustainable benchmark; enforcement mechanisms score below 1.0 even at their best, since monitoring itself costs something (see docs/metrics.md). With a second resource on, the layout above switches to one card per pool plus a Final card: Harvest and Welfare efficiency stay combined across both pools (they're additive quantities, judged against both pools' combined sustainable yield) — but each pool's own stock and sustainability get their own card, since the two can and do fail independently (E20's own finding: the fragile pool collapses well before the reliable one). Reporting one combined number there would hide exactly the kind of collapse this project's own methodology says to report separately, not average away.

Complexity: does the near-optimal set grow?

The equifinality question this project's thesis direction is actually about: make the world structurally richer (combine axes, not just turn up one existing dial) — does the count of different approaches that still reach a near-optimal outcome grow? Boundary builds on Groups, and Network builds on Reputation, so switching either on turns its prerequisite on too; Resources is independent and combines with any of the others. Reputation adds reputation_cooperator as a 6th composable type (harmless no-op for every composition that doesn't happen to use it — see the comment above CX_DIVERSITY_TYPES); Network additionally restricts its partner selection to a fixed ring lattice instead of a fresh random draw each round (ADR-0014/0015). Resources adds a second, fragile pool (g=0.2 vs. 0.4) and sweeps the arrangement dropdown above — concentrated (every agent ignores pool B entirely — reproduces the single-pool numbers, minus the combined-yield welfare denominator), balanced (every agent, including any sanctioning ones, splits its effort 0.5/0.5), or specialized (each composition's own agents split roughly half-and-half into pool-A-only and pool-B-only specialists, by agent index within each type) — the welfare threshold is always judged against both pools' combined sustainable yield (E20, ADR-0016).

What this project is about

A small, reproducible model to study when and why decentralized cooperation emerges, and how fragile it is. Thirteen experiments (E1–E13) build one story; every regime above is a slice of it, and every claim below is a runnable, seed-reproducible result — not a new algorithm, but a systematic, literature-grounded measurement.

In calm conditions — the mechanism ladder

  • selfish →the pool is emptied in one round (tragedy of the commons).
  • cooperation →sustains it — but is exploited once free-riders appear.
  • reciprocity →starves free-riders (fairer) yet the retaliation spiral collapses the resource.
  • enforcement →caps everyone at a quota — the only rule that protects both resource and fairness (monitors pay a cost).

Under disturbance — resilience

  • E8 —information decides recovery. The same cooperators recover from a shock if they can see the depleted stock, and collapse if blind. Enforcement alone does not help.
  • E9 —with free-riders present, enforcement is also required to recover — observation lets the pool climb back, enforcement decides how many free-riders it can carry.
  • E10 —when agents fail, who fails decides: losing the enforcer collapses the commons, a cooperator is harmless, a free-rider helps. Enforcement is a single point of failure.
  • ↳Try the 💥 Shock and 🔌/🧩 failure regimes above to see each case play out.

The throughline: cooperation needs information (to self-correct) and, against free-riders, a binding rule (enforcement). Resilience needs the same two together — neither alone survives a disturbed, mixed commons. This reproduces, in a minimal model, classic results from Hardin, Ostrom, and the evolution-of-cooperation literature.
