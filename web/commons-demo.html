Common-pool resource · agent-based model

Watch a shared resource live or die

Eight agents draw from one renewable pool each round. Whether it is sustained or collapses depends on who is in the group, what they can see, whether anyone enforces a limit, and whether it survives a shock. Pick a regime or adjust the dials, then press run — this is a live, faithful port of the research engine below.

What this project is about

A small, reproducible model to study when and why decentralized cooperation emerges, and how fragile it is. Thirteen experiments (E1–E13) build one story; every regime above is a slice of it, and every claim below is a runnable, seed-reproducible result — not a new algorithm, but a systematic, literature-grounded measurement.

In calm conditions — the mechanism ladder

  • selfish →the pool is emptied in one round (tragedy of the commons).
  • cooperation →sustains it — but is exploited once free-riders appear.
  • reciprocity →starves free-riders (fairer) yet the retaliation spiral collapses the resource.
  • enforcement →caps everyone at a quota — the only rule that protects both resource and fairness (monitors pay a cost).

Under disturbance — resilience

  • E8 —information decides recovery. The same cooperators recover from a shock if they can see the depleted stock, and collapse if blind. Enforcement alone does not help.
  • E9 —with free-riders present, enforcement is also required to recover — observation lets the pool climb back, enforcement decides how many free-riders it can carry.
  • E10 —when agents fail, who fails decides: losing the enforcer collapses the commons, a cooperator is harmless, a free-rider helps. Enforcement is a single point of failure.
  • ↳Try the 💥 Shock and 🔌/🧩 failure regimes above to see each case play out.

The throughline: cooperation needs information (to self-correct) and, against free-riders, a binding rule (enforcement). Resilience needs the same two together — neither alone survives a disturbed, mixed commons. This reproduces, in a minimal model, classic results from Hardin, Ostrom, and the evolution-of-cooperation literature.
