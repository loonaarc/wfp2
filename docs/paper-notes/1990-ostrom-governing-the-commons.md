## Citation
Ostrom, E. (1990). *Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for
Collective Action*. Cambridge University Press. (~280 pp.)

## Research Problem
Contrary to the "tragedy of the commons" (Hardin 1968) and the prisoner's-dilemma
prediction that shared resources are inevitably over-exploited, many communities have
*successfully* governed common-pool resources for generations without top-down
privatization or state control. What institutional arrangements make self-governance
of a commons endure?

## Why the Problem Is Difficult
_(read the book)_ — the free-rider and collective-action problems; monitoring and
enforcement are themselves costly public goods; credible commitment.

## Proposed Method / Contribution
Comparative case analysis of long-enduring (and failed) CPR institutions, from which
Ostrom distills **eight design principles** common to enduring commons:

1. **Clearly defined boundaries** (of the resource and who may use it).
2. **Congruence** between appropriation/provision rules and local conditions.
3. **Collective-choice arrangements** — most affected can modify the rules.
4. **Monitoring** — of resource condition and user behaviour, by accountable monitors.
5. **Graduated sanctions** for rule violations.
6. **Conflict-resolution mechanisms** — cheap, fast, accessible.
7. **Minimal recognition of rights to organize** (by external authorities).
8. **Nested enterprises** — layered governance for larger systems.
